For buyers

Buy with a plan, not pressure.

The goal is not simply to win an offer. It is to buy the right property at a price and risk level you can live with after closing.

1. Set the real budget

Mortgage approval is only one boundary. We also look at land transfer tax, closing costs, property tax, condo fees where applicable, utilities, insurance, maintenance and a realistic emergency buffer.

2. Compare neighbourhood fit

Commute, schools, transit, future development, housing stock, lot size, age of construction and local resale patterns can materially change the value of two homes with similar list prices.

3. Read the comparable sales properly

Comparable sales are useful only when the property type, condition, size, location, timing and competitive context are genuinely comparable. I use comps to frame a range and negotiation strategy rather than pretending one sale determines value.

4. Decide conditions before emotions take over

Financing, inspection, status certificate review and other conditions should reflect the property and your risk tolerance. Removing a condition can make an offer stronger, but it also transfers risk to you.

5. Plan the closing before signing

Deposit timing, lawyer, insurance, utilities, lender requirements and moving logistics should be understood before the last week. A smooth closing is usually the result of early coordination.

First-time buyer focus: I regularly help buyers compare areas, understand total carrying costs and work through the anxiety that comes with a first purchase.
